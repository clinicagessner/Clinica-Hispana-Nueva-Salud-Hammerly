You're standing in the grocery line when heat climbs from your chest to your face, your neck gets soaked and you want to peel off your sweater even though it's cold outside. At three in the morning it happens again: you wake up on a damp pillow, kick the covers off, pull them back on, and sleep is gone for the night. Plenty of women come to see us with that story and one question: "Is this menopause already?"

The answer usually has some nuance. Here's what's going on in your body, what helps at home with the heat and the sleepless nights, which signs shouldn't be brushed off and which checkups are worth catching up on at this stage.

## Perimenopause and menopause aren't the same thing

**Menopause** is confirmed once you've gone twelve months in a row without a period, with no other reason behind it such as pregnancy or a birth control method that stops bleeding. For most women it arrives around age 50, though it can come several years earlier or later.

The years leading up to it are called **perimenopause**, and that's when the symptoms tend to start. The ovaries make less estrogen, but not in a smooth line: some months bring sharp ups and downs. That's why periods may come early, come late, skip a month or change in flow, and symptoms show up and fade without warning.

One detail surprises a lot of women: as long as you still get periods, even irregular ones, pregnancy is still possible. If you're not planning a baby, talk about [birth control](/en/services/anticonceptivos) with the Clínica Hispana Nueva Salud Hammerly medical team until a full year without a period is confirmed.

## What it feels like, beyond the hot flashes

Hot flashes are the best-known sign, but not the only one. At this stage it's also common to have:

- **Night sweats**, which are hot flashes that arrive while you sleep and wake you up drenched.
- **Broken sleep or insomnia**, with or without the heat.
- **Mood changes**: irritability, sadness, crying easily or a sense of anxiety.
- **Forgetfulness and trouble focusing**, which often ease once sleep improves.
- **Vaginal dryness** and discomfort during sex, or needing to pee more often.
- **Achy joints**, drier skin and weight shifting, especially to the waist.

Not every woman gets all of it, or with the same intensity. Some move through the transition barely noticing; others go years without a full night's rest.

## Turning down the heat

A hot flash lasts a few minutes but feels endless. These steps help many women:

1. **Dress in layers** of cotton or breathable fabric you can pull off quickly.
2. **Keep a fan and a glass of cold water nearby**; small sips as the heat starts can help.
3. **Notice what sets it off.** Coffee, alcohol, very spicy food, hot drinks and stress often spark a flash. Jot down for two weeks when they hit; many women find a pattern.
4. **Breathe slowly** when you feel one starting: breathe in for a count of four and out for a count of six, several times.
5. **If you smoke, this is a good time to quit.** Smoking is linked to stronger hot flashes and more risk for the heart and bones.

## Sleeping better when the night runs hot

- Keep the bedroom cool and dark; a fan aimed at the bed makes a difference.
- Use cotton sheets and light pajamas, and leave a dry change of clothes by the bed so you can switch without turning on the light.
- Eat dinner early and light, and stop coffee after noon.
- Go to bed and get up at the same time, weekends included.
- If you've been tossing for more than twenty minutes, get up, do something calm in low light and go back to bed once you feel sleepy. Leave the phone outside the bedroom.

If you snore loudly, wake up gasping or your partner notices you stop breathing for moments, mention it at your visit: not every sleepless night at this age comes from hormones.

## Be careful with remedies passed from hand to hand

At family gatherings and on social media you'll find teas, "natural" capsules and creams that promise to end hot flashes. Some herbal products can interfere with medicines you already take for blood pressure, blood sugar or your thyroid. Before trying any of them, bring it to your visit in its original package.

There are also prescription treatments, hormonal and non-hormonal, for when symptoms take a real toll on your life. Whether one suits you is something the medical team decides after reviewing your history, your blood pressure and your family background; borrowing a friend's pills or using something brought from another country is never a good idea. ## Signs that aren't just "part of getting older"

Get checked soon, without waiting for your routine visit, if:

- **You bleed again** after a full year without a period, even just spotting.
- Periods become so frequent or so heavy that they disrupt your life.
- Sadness or low mood lasts for weeks, or you have thoughts of hurting yourself.
- You lose weight without trying, your heart races at rest or your hands shake: an overactive [thyroid](/en/services/tiroides) can feel like one long hot flash.

Call **911** or go to the emergency room if bleeding is so heavy that you feel dizzy or faint, if sudden severe pelvic pain appears, or if a so-called hot flash comes with chest pressure, shortness of breath, nausea or a cold sweat.

## The checkups worth catching up on

The transition is a good moment to review what sometimes gets put off:

| Checkup | Why it matters at this stage |
|---|---|
| Blood pressure | It tends to rise after menopause and rarely causes symptoms |
| Glucose and cholesterol | Hormonal change and weight around the waist move these numbers |
| Thyroid panel | Thyroid problems get mistaken for fatigue, heat, insomnia or mood swings |
| Complete blood count | Picks up anemia if periods were heavy in recent years |
| Pap smear | Repeated based on your age and past results |
| Mammogram | Ask for the order during your women's checkup |

At our [gynecology visit](/en/services/ginecologia) we go over your symptoms and your period history and tell you when your next Pap smear is due. [Blood tests](/en/services/examenes-sangre) are drawn right at the clinic; glucose and cholesterol call for fasting, with plain water allowed. If your blood pressure, sugar or cholesterol come back high, follow-up continues in our [chronic conditions](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas) program.

Bones also lose strength faster after menopause. Walking, exercises with weights or resistance bands, milk, yogurt, cheese, leafy greens and a bit of sunshine all help. Ask at your visit when a bone density scan makes sense for you; if you need one, we'll point you to where it can be done.
