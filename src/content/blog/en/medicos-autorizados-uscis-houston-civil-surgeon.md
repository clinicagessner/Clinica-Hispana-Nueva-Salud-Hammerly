If your immigration process requires a medical exam, you'll hear the term *civil surgeon*. Choosing this doctor well matters more than it seems: an exam done by the wrong physician simply doesn't count. Here's what a civil surgeon is, how to verify one, and what to expect from the visit.

## What is a civil surgeon?

A civil surgeon is a **physician authorized by USCIS** — U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services — to perform the immigration medical exam ([Form I-693](/en/blog/guia-examen-medico-inmigracion-i693-houston)) inside the United States. The name is confusing: they don't "operate" on anyone; it's simply the official title USCIS gives to physicians designated for this process.

Not every doctor qualifies. Your regular physician, however good, cannot sign a valid I-693 without the USCIS designation.

## Why does choosing well matter so much?

Because USCIS **only accepts the form completed, signed and sealed by a civil surgeon**. If a non-authorized doctor performs it:

- The form will be rejected, even if the exam itself was done correctly
- You pay twice: once for the invalid exam and again for the repeat
- Your case can be delayed weeks or months over an avoidable RFE (request for evidence)

In a process where every document counts, this is a completely preventable mistake.

## What the civil surgeon does during the exam

1. **Reviews your medical and vaccine history**, including records you bring from your home country
2. **Performs the general physical exam**
3. **Orders the tests USCIS requires**, such as tuberculosis screening and the blood and urine tests that apply to your age
4. **Administers any vaccines you're missing**, at the same clinic when possible
5. **Completes and signs Form I-693** and hands it to you in a **sealed envelope** you must not open

For the full breakdown of tests, vaccines and what to bring on the day of your visit, see our [step-by-step I-693 exam guide](/en/blog/guia-examen-medico-inmigracion-i693-houston).

## How to verify a doctor is authorized

- **Use the official USCIS locator:** uscis.gov offers the *"Find a Doctor"* tool, where you can search civil surgeons by ZIP code
- **Ask the clinic directly** whether a USCIS-authorized physician performs the exam and whether they deliver the sealed envelope
- **Be wary of anyone who can't confirm it clearly** or offers to "fix" the form without an exam

## What else to ask before you go

1. **Do you serve patients in Spanish?** The exam includes questions about your health history; understanding them well prevents errors on the form.
2. **Do you give vaccines the same day?** If you're missing one, solving it in the same visit saves you trips.
3. **How much does it cost and what does the price include?** A serious clinic tells you upfront, lab tests included.
4. **Do I need an appointment?** Some places book weeks out; others, like our clinic, see you [with no appointment](/en/walk-in).

## Frequently asked questions

**Are a civil surgeon and a panel physician the same thing?**
No. The *civil surgeon* performs the exam inside the United States (for example, for adjustment of status). The *panel physician* performs it abroad, for consular processing. If your process is inside the U.S., you need a civil surgeon.

**Does passing the medical exam guarantee my case is approved?**
No; it's just one requirement. The civil surgeon certifies your health status, and USCIS evaluates the whole case. For legal questions, talk to your immigration attorney.

**Can I open the sealed envelope to check that everything is right?**
Don't open it: an opened envelope can invalidate the form. Ask the clinic for a **copy** of your I-693 for your records — it's your right, and we always provide one.

**What if I'm missing a vaccine?**
The civil surgeon reviews your record and administers what's missing for your age. At our clinic we do it in the same visit whenever possible.
