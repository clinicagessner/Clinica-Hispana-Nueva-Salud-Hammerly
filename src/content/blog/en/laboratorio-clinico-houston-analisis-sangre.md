A blood test can say a lot about your health, even before any symptom appears. Diabetes, high cholesterol and thyroid problems often advance silently for years — and all three are caught with a blood sample. Here's which tests exist, what each one is for and how to get them in Houston with no appointment, in Spanish.

## Why get blood tests?

- **Catch problems early**, while they're still easy and inexpensive to treat
- **Monitor a chronic condition** like [diabetes, high blood pressure or cholesterol](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas)
- **Meet a requirement:** [immigration exam](/en/services/examenes-inmigracion), work or school
- **Explain vague symptoms:** constant tiredness, hair loss, weight changes — the answer is often in your blood

## The most common tests and what each detects

- **Complete blood count (CBC):** evaluates red cells, white cells and platelets; detects anemia and infections
- **Blood chemistry:** measures glucose and kidney and liver function
- **Lipid panel:** total cholesterol, HDL, LDL and triglycerides — key for heart health
- **Glucose and hemoglobin A1c:** diabetes diagnosis and control; A1c shows your average sugar over the past 3 months
- **[Thyroid tests](/en/services/tiroides):** check whether your thyroid is over- or under-working — a frequent cause of fatigue and weight changes
- **[Vitamin B12](/en/blog/vitamina-b12-beneficios-inyecciones-houston):** deficiency is common and explains fatigue, tingling and memory issues
- **Hormone panel and [PSA for men](/en/services/salud-hombre):** prostate health and testosterone
- **[STD testing](/en/services/enfermedades-transmision-sexual):** with complete privacy

Not sure which one you need? You don't have to be: at your visit we guide you based on your age, symptoms and history.

## Do I need to fast?

It depends on the test. Glucose and the lipid panel usually require **8 to 12 hours of fasting** (water is fine); CBC and thyroid tests normally don't. When in doubt, call us before coming and we'll tell you exactly how to prepare so you don't have to come back another day.

## How often should I get blood work?

For healthy adults, **once a year** is a good rule of thumb. If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, cholesterol or family history, your doctor may recommend more frequent checks — every 3 to 6 months — to adjust treatment in time.
